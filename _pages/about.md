As of September 2026, I am in Grenoble, France exploring coastal applications for the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) satellite mission and developing algorithms to optimize SWOT data to study nearshore processes. Some the specific things I am working on right now on this topic is understanding how SWOT measurements are affected by various wave conditions and using the SWOT pixel cloud data to estimate wave setup.

I did my PhD at Northeastern University in Boston (USA) in the Civil and Environmental Engineering Department under the direction of Julia Hopkins and Ed Beighley. My dissertation titled _Advances in Remote Measurements of Nearshore Dynamics_ combined in-situ data with remote observations of coastal processes to quantify key relationships. These relationships make it possible to estimate coastal processes like orbital velocities beneath waves with data obtained exclusively from remote sensing platforms. The nearshore is a dynamic and highly energetic zone where collecting data is extremely difficult. But this region is also a critical interface between the oceans and civilization making it increasingly important to understand, monitor, and manage this region as coastal populations grow. 

I grew up in New Jersey (USA) and did my undergraduate studies in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in Troy, New York.
